Restia

A self-hosted workspace that brings model chat, projects, study tools, and documents together.

By Mithul Sourav20 July 20262 min read

On this page

  1. Workspace and model choices
  2. Calendar and project planning
  3. Self-hosting and collaboration

I built Restia because I wanted model conversations to sit alongside the projects, files, and tasks they help with. I explored a web interface, a desktop-style dashboard, and Python services before combining them in a self-hosted workspace.

Workspace and model choices

I gave projects their own boards and documents. The study area holds goals and focus sessions, and chat sits alongside that work. Building the early versions separately helped me see which things need to last beyond a conversation, such as a document or a decision saved to a project.

I kept model selection separate from the workspace layout. Restia can use a local model or a hosted provider while the project’s files and tasks stay in the same place.

The early prototypes gave me different places to put the same information. I settled on saving decisions and documents inside projects because they need to be easy to find after a chat ends. I kept provider choice on the chat side of the workspace so changing models leaves the board and files where they are.

Calendar and project planning

The board keeps track of work that needs to move through stages. The calendar is where I put work tied to a date or time, such as a test slot or a review. In this example, the calibration and review are calendar events, while the preparation and analysis stay on the project board.

Self-hosting and collaboration

The files and database stay on the owner’s infrastructure. I also built a way to share a project between Restia installations. The owner can grant viewer or editor access, which let me handle collaboration at the project level.

Sharing between installations raised two separate questions: who serves the file and who can change it. I kept the owner’s installation in charge of the file and made viewing and editing separate permissions. That lets someone read a shared project before the owner decides whether to give them write access.
