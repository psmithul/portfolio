What should the rover do when it starts shaking?

How I’d compare slowing down with changing the suspension, and where a controller could help.

By Mithul Sourav30 September 20262 min read

I started thinking about this while working on our rough-terrain rover. If the camera starts shaking, slowing down is an obvious thing to try. We also designed the suspension so we could adjust the tension in its springs. I wanted to know whether that would help the rover stay steady at the same speed.

I’d start with a fixed suspension setting and drive the same stretch at a few speeds. Then I’d repeat it at the same speeds with a different setting. I’d look at the vibration on the chassis alongside what the wheels are doing. That gives me something to compare before I ask a controller to adjust anything.

For holding a set speed, I’d start with PID and watch how the motors respond under load. The suspension is a separate decision. One bump can shake the body for a moment. I’m more interested in what happens when the shaking continues, because that’s when it might be worth changing the setting.

I’d consider a learned model if I had enough runs on different surfaces to see whether it can predict when a setting will help. Right now the suspension is at the CAD and control design stage. I still want to see what changes on the ground.
