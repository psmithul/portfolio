Mikaverse

Personal game project

I built Mikaverse as a Minecraft Java world for a small group playing on Fabric. I wanted a tower on the skyline or a railway leaving a settlement to give players a reason to set out, and a shared home to give them a place to bring their finds.

Railways, settlements, and inscriptions

I used tracks and visible landmarks to lead players between places. Settlements give context to nearby inscriptions, and discoveries such as the archive mural put parts of the story into the world itself.

I gave the signs consistent meanings across inscriptions. A player who recognises a sign from one trip can use it as a clue somewhere else. That makes learning the script part of exploring the world.

At a crossing stone, a player can use the sign key to choose a reading. If the reading is wrong, the game points them back to part of the inscription. A correct reading reveals a local custom. I wanted players to work out the meaning from clues they could find in the world.

I kept the sign set small enough for a player to recognise signs in a different order on another trip. The hint on a wrong reading sends the player back to part of the inscription, so there is a reason to look at the clue again before trying another answer.

Rubbings and the shared library

I made rubbings into items players can carry home and compare with other finds. Books and rubbings can be kept together in the library, so someone who stayed home can still see what the group found. Designing the trip through to that point gave the discovery a use beyond the place where it was made.

I separated finding an inscription from figuring it out. A rubbing gives the player something they can carry back even if they do not know the reading yet. The library then gives the group a place to put two finds side by side and see which signs recur.
