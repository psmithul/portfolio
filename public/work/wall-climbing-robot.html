Wall-Climbing Robot

ISTE NITK · CLUTCH team project · October 2024 — March 2025

We wanted a small robot that could climb a concrete wall and send back video for crack inspection. We chose a ducted fan to press the robot against the wall and four driven wheels to move it along the surface.

Keeping grip on the wall

On a vertical surface, the wheels have to support the robot’s weight through friction. The fan pushes the chassis towards the wall, increasing the force at the tyres. The wheel motors turn that grip into motion along the wall. If the tyres have less grip on a particular surface, the fan needs to press harder to support the same weight.

Our sizing plan was to estimate how much thrust we needed using the robot’s weight and an assumed friction coefficient for each surface. We also planned an ANSYS airflow study to see how moving the fan closer to the wall would change the thrust it produced.

Driving the robot

The control design used a wireless remote, an Arduino, and motor drivers for the four DC wheel motors. A separate electronic speed controller set the speed of the ducted fan. This gave the operator a way to adjust the force into the wall independently of the direction of travel.

Looking for cracks

A wireless camera on the chassis would send video of the concrete surface back to the operator. The proposal identified percolation-based image processing as a way to look for connected crack-like regions in those frames.
