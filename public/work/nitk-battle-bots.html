Combat Robot Development

Team mechanical design · NITK Battle Bots

At NITK Battle Bots, we worked on a 3 lb robot with a one-piece chassis and the 15 kg GN4RLY 2.5. We assembled GN4RLY 2.5 in the Robocon Lab and competed with it at the event shown below.

Weight classes and build constraints

On the 3 lb build, the chassis had to hold the electronics in a small space with as few separate parts as possible. The 15 kg build had more room, but the assemblies were larger and we had to work within the event’s weight limit and our fabrication budget.

3 lb chassis and assembly

We used the compact layout of Jelly Baby as a reference for the 3 lb build. We chose a one-piece chassis to reduce the number of parts we had to make and fit. Jelly Baby’s NHRL profile.

The first photo shows what the one-piece chassis looked like before we fitted anything. In the second, the battery and electronics occupy much of that open space. Comparing the two made it easier to see why we had to consider access for wiring and assembly while laying out the chassis.

GN4RLY 2.5 build and competition

Sponsorship let us use stronger materials on GN4RLY 2.5. At the bench, we had to bring the frame, drivetrain, and electrical assemblies together in an order we could build and service.

Event constraints

We reused an assembly from an earlier design. The robot’s weight and that older assembly held us back at the event, and we did not advance to the later rounds. The remaining budget was too small to fabricate a redesign. The experience made the cost of carrying over an older assembly clear to me. A part can be available and still be difficult to fit or repair in the complete build.

The workbench assembly told us whether the frame, drivetrain, and electronics fitted together. The event exposed a different constraint: we could assemble the robot with the parts we had, but the complete build still carried the limitations of the older design.
