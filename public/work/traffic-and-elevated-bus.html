Smart Traffic Light System and Transit Elevated Bus Model

Two electronic model builds · Sep 2018 — Jan 2019

I built a model traffic junction and a separate elevated-bus model. I wired the circuits by hand and used logic gates to control their sequences.

Early model-scale projects in circuit wiring, logic, and debugging.

Where the two models started

I was interested in how a model can move through a visible sequence using a circuit I had wired myself. At a traffic junction, I could see the order in which the lights changed. The bus model gave me another sequence to build and observe.

I made the two models with separate control circuits. That let me work through the order of events in each model and trace its wiring independently.

Wiring the traffic-light sequence

I built a model junction and used hand-wired logic gates to control the order of the signals. I traced the wiring until the signals followed the intended sequence.

I checked the output at each point in the sequence against the light that was supposed to be on. Because the result was visible on the model, a wrong output gave me a concrete place to start tracing a gate or connection.

Building a separate bus circuit

The transit elevated-bus model had its own control circuit. I worked through wiring and logic faults on that build too, checking that the model could repeat its sequence.

For the bus, I used its own wiring and repeated the sequence after working through the faults. The two builds gave me practice translating an intended order into a circuit and checking it against what the model actually did.

What I learned

I learned to debug a sequence one transition at a time. Watching where it first went wrong gave me a smaller set of connections to inspect, and checking that it repeated gave me a better sense of whether the fix held up.

Working through the two circuits also helped me distinguish the intended behaviour, the logic that represents it, and the wiring that makes that logic run.
