Physics-Informed Multi-Fidelity FEM Surrogate for Reaction-Wheel-Induced Spacecraft Microvibration and Payload Jitter

Computational study · 5–20 Hz reduced-model pilot · Aug — Oct 2026

I wanted to see how vibration at a spacecraft reaction wheel reaches a camera elsewhere on the structure. I built a reduced finite-element model and tested whether neural surrogates could help me compare mount settings across 5–20 Hz.

I used the reduced model to train the surrogates and checked selected points against the full COMSOL model.

Pointing problem

A camera support can flex even after the spacecraft’s overall rotation is removed. I focused on the resulting structural tilt to see how much the wheel mounts and the structure contribute to pointing motion.

COMSOL model and response

I modeled a free-floating spacecraft in COMSOL. At each of four wheels I applied two transverse force inputs and two moment inputs, for 16 inputs in total. I estimated camera tilt from the average motion at two stations on the support, about 0.48 m apart, after removing the spacecraft’s overall rotation.

I calculated the steady harmonic response one input and frequency at a time. That gave me a response for each load direction and let me compare the reduced model against the same camera and bench outputs.

Reduced-order model

The original mesh had about 1.01 million volume elements. I reduced the model to 36 natural modes for repeated calculations. The smaller basis missed some bending near the camera support, so I added 16 corrections and used the resulting 52-coordinate model as the training reference.

Surrogate comparison

I held out whole structural configurations. Frequency points from one curve share the same structure, so splitting them across training and test sets would give the model information about the test configuration.

The multilayer perceptron (MLP) came closest to the 52-coordinate reference in this comparison. The table gives mean frequency-integrated complex-response errors on three unseen configurations, with all 16 inputs tested on each.

Response MLP DeepONet
Camera displacement 0.139% 0.342%
Optical bench displacement 0.171% 0.378%
Structural tilt 0.261% 0.701%

Pretraining and equilibrium loss

Pretraining on the 36-coordinate model helped when I had few 52-coordinate cases. The benefit disappeared with the largest training set. In a separate single-input experiment, an equilibrium term in the training loss lowered the balanced residual by 9.23%. Its effect on resonance prediction was mixed.

Mount stiffness screening

I screened 17 mount stiffness settings. The surrogate suggested a 10% increase. I compared that setting with the original using the 52-coordinate model on 36 separate scenarios. The worst response in that set changed by only 0.00589%, while the largest neural peak error was 7.61%.

I kept the original stiffness. The comparison showed me why peak error matters when the candidate designs are close. The surrogate could narrow the search, and the separate numerical comparison established the size of the proposed benefit.

Full-model comparison

I compared the surrogate predictions with 14 full-model points across six configurations. Four points came from configurations held out of the original comparison. In a separate wheel-speed study, changing the mesh shifted the peak amplitude near 5,800 rpm by 0.33%.

The reported tilt is a modeled structural response. The full COMSOL comparisons cover the selected cases.
