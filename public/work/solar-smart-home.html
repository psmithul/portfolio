Arduino-Based Smart Home with Solar Power

Working electronic model · Sep 2019 — Jan 2020

I built a small Arduino smart-home model, wired its sensors, and tested it with a solar cell as a second power source.

A working model with integrated sensors and a solar-power option.

Why build a model house?

I wanted to see how a small automated system connects what it senses to what it does. A model house gave me a manageable way to put sensors, controller logic, and power in the same build.

I also wanted to understand what changes when the same circuit gets its power from a different source. That is where the photovoltaic cell came into the project.

Getting the sensors to work together

I wired the sensors into an Arduino and wrote the logic for the model’s automated functions. I debugged the connections and code until the inputs and outputs worked together.

I worked through the signal path from each sensor to the Arduino and then to the model’s response. Keeping the connections and the program in mind together helped me narrow down which part of the path was responsible when a response did not appear.

Once the inputs and outputs worked together, I could check a control decision by watching the model respond to a sensor input.

Trying solar power

I added a photovoltaic cell as a second way to power the model and tested it on that supply. It was an early chance for me to work through the power, wiring, and control of one system.

I kept the solar cell as a second supply option for the same model. Testing it with the sensors and controller connected brought the power source into the same system I was already debugging.

What I learned

The build taught me to look at power, wiring, and software together. Debugging the Arduino by tracing what entered and left each part made it easier to tell whether I was dealing with a connection or a control decision.

Trying the second supply helped me think about a sensor and controller as a physical circuit that needs power as well as a program that needs to run.
