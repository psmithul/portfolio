Four-Bar Linkage Door-Opening Mechanism

Working prototype · Team build · Feb — Apr 2024

Supervised by Dr. Khyati Verma.

Turn a crank and the door swings open. We built a working mechanism to see how four links could make that happen. Once the links and pivots were on the frame, we could turn the input by hand and watch the door follow.

Try the linkage motion ↓

Making the door swing

We made the links, set the two fixed pivots on the frame, and joined the moving parts. The input crank moves the connecting link, which turns the rocker at the door hinge. The frame holds the two fixed pivots and acts as the fourth link.

Once it was assembled, we turned the crank through the stroke and watched where the door started, how it moved between the stops, and where it ended up. It made the role of the pivots much easier to see. The door follows an arc because the rocker and the door rotate around the same hinge.

Two-lid CAD study

I reconstructed a version with two connecting rods on one crank. Each rod has its own geometry, so I compared how far the lids opened, how much input effort the motion required, and how sensitive the result was to small changes in dimensions.

The first crank took one lid to 24° and the other to 38°. Giving the crank more travel made one lid turn back before the stroke ended. Changing both the linkage geometry and the crank’s travel brought the revised model to 75° on each side.

The two connecting rods are blue and amber. Both pivot on the same pin at the end of the purple crank. Two-lid analysis.

Comparing layouts at the same opening angle

Once both lids could reach 75°, I compared layouts on two things I still cared about: how far apart the lid angles got during the stroke, and how much torque the crank needed. In one CAD study, one candidate peaked at about 7.0 N·m with a 7.0° maximum mismatch. Another narrowed that gap to 6.7° but raised the torque to about 8.7 N·m. That small improvement in synchronisation came at a noticeable cost in input torque.

I checked the three shortlisted layouts for interference at 361 positions across the stroke. None showed unintended interference at those sampled positions. The torque and angle figures came from the model, and the interference check was limited to the sampled CAD positions.
