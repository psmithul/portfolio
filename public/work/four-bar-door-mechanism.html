Four-Bar Linkage Door-Opening Mechanism

Team build · Feb — Apr 2024

We built a four-bar door opener to see how one turning input could produce a particular door path. We worked back from the desired motion to the pivot positions and link lengths. I later used CAD to study a two-lid version driven by one crank.

Link geometry

We started with the fixed pivots and compared how the moving links fit together in the closed and open positions. The link lengths and pivot positions determine how far the door opens and what path it takes between those positions.

Build and motion check

We made the links and pivots, assembled the opener, and turned the input to see where the door moved and stopped. Watching the full motion made the effect of pivot placement easier to see than it had been in the layout drawing.

Two-lid CAD study

I reconstructed a version with two connecting rods on one crank. Each rod has its own geometry, so I compared how far the lids opened, how much input effort the motion required, and how sensitive the result was to small changes in dimensions.

With the first short crank, both lids opened less than I wanted, and their final angles differed by 14°. I tried giving that layout more travel, but one lid started to reverse before the sweep ended. I changed the linkage geometry and the range of crank travel together. In the revised CAD motion both lids reached 75°.

Two-lid analysis.

Comparing layouts at the same opening angle

Once both lids could reach 75°, I compared layouts on two things I still cared about: how far apart the lid angles got during the stroke, and how much torque the crank needed. In one CAD study, one candidate peaked at about 7.0 N·m with a 7.0° maximum mismatch. Another narrowed that gap to 6.7° but raised the torque to about 8.7 N·m. That small improvement in synchronisation came at a noticeable cost in input torque.

I checked the three shortlisted layouts for interference at 361 positions across the stroke. None showed unintended interference at those sampled positions. The torque and angle figures came from the model, and the interference check was limited to the sampled CAD positions.

Candidate comparison and CAD checks.
