Terrain-Adaptive Leaf Cleaning Module

NITK · Virtual Instrumentation team project · Jul 2026 — Present

We designed a cleaning module for wet and dry leaves on uneven ground. It mounts beneath a collection hopper. We completed the six-unit CAD assembly and demonstrated one printed tiller unit outdoors.

Ground contact

Wet leaves sit close to the ground, particularly in low spots. A pickup set at one height can pass over those leaves and scrape soil from higher patches. We wanted the cleaning head to accommodate changes in height across its width.

Tiller design

The mounting plate carries six identical tiller units. Each has a two-stage telescoping shaft, a guide collar, and a roller held in a yoke. The roller contacts the ground while the shaft can extend and retract vertically.

We studied the motion of one unit first. In the six-unit assembly we checked its spacing from its neighbours and how the repeated units attach to the plate.

The single-unit model gave us one shaft and roller to check. Repeating it under the shared plate turned that into a spacing problem: each yoke and roller needed room to move beside the next one. We kept the unit geometry identical so the same printed parts could be used across the assembly.

Fabrication

Repeating one tiller design reduced the number of unique parts. We drew the single unit and the full assembly, then used a bill of materials to identify the PETG parts and the purchased hardware, including rollers, springs, and rack-and-pinion components.

Motion study and prototype

In CAD we checked extension, retraction, and where the roller reaches the ground line. The outdoor demonstration gave us a physical unit to compare with that motion.

These checks showed the movement of the printed unit and how the roller met the ground. They did not measure how many leaves the assembly collected or how much soil it picked up.
