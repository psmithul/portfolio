UAV Vibration Analysis & Isolation

Product Intern · Vayu Aerospace Pvt. Ltd., Bengaluru · 1 June – 15 July 2026

I compared three ways to mount a UAV flight controller at Vayu Aerospace. Ground motor tests helped us choose an elastomer-isolated tray.

The goal was to reduce the motor vibration reaching the flight controller’s inertial measurement unit (IMU) while keeping the electronics accessible.

Original project images and measured vibration values are redacted under a nondisclosure agreement (NDA). The 3D renders illustrate the mounting concepts.

Comparing the mounts in ANSYS

I traced the vibration from the motors through the airframe to the flight controller’s IMU. In ANSYS Mechanical, I used modal analysis to see where each assembly might resonate, then compared how motion from the airframe could reach the tray. The rigid mount provided a baseline for the elastomer-isolated and suspended concepts.

Fitting the mounts into the aircraft

After shortlisting the two isolation mounts, I checked the manufactured parts for fit, connector access, and fastener clearance. I kept the controller orientation consistent so we could compare the same IMU axes across mounts. I also checked cable slack because a tight cable can carry vibration around the isolator.

Ground-test results

We logged the flight controller’s IMU during ground motor runs. In MATLAB, I selected comparable steady portions of each run and compared the overall vibration level using RMS. I used FFT spectra to see which frequencies contributed to it.

Both isolation mounts reduced measured IMU vibration relative to the rigid baseline. The team selected the elastomer-isolated modular tray after weighing the test results and ANSYS analysis alongside fit, wiring, and assembly.

The actual vibration measurements are confidential under the NDA.
