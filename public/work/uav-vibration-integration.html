UAV Vibration Analysis & Isolation

Product Intern · Vayu Aerospace Pvt. Ltd., Bengaluru · 1 June – 15 July 2026

At Vayu Aerospace I compared three ways to mount a UAV flight controller. My goal was to reduce the motor vibration reaching its inertial measurement unit (IMU) while keeping the electronics accessible.

Original project images and measured vibration values are redacted under a nondisclosure agreement (NDA). The 3D renders illustrate the mounting concepts.

Vibration path

I started with the airframe, flight controller, and onboard logs. The controller uses accelerometer and gyroscope readings to estimate how the aircraft is moving. Vibration from the motors travels through the airframe and mount and appears in those same readings. I focused on what happens between the frame and the controller tray.

Mount design and modal analysis

I modeled the tray, supports, and frame attachments in ANSYS Mechanical. I kept the electronics mass and its position comparable across concepts. I represented fastened joints and compliant supports separately to keep the intended isolation path in each model.

The rigid mount was the baseline. The elastomer tray added compliance and damping in a compact package. The suspended mount allowed more relative motion, so I paid particular attention to rocking and clearance. Modal analysis showed the motion each arrangement allowed, and the response comparison showed how excitation from the frame could reach the tray.

The modes gave me a way to distinguish vertical motion from rocking or twisting. That mattered for the clearance check because a tray can move towards a nearby part at one corner as it rotates.

Hardware integration

We shortlisted the elastomer and suspended mounts for hardware testing. I checked the manufactured parts against the installation space, including fastener fit, connector access, and clearance. I kept the controller in the same orientation so I could compare the same IMU axes across mounts.

A taut cable or contact with a nearby part can carry vibration around an isolator. I checked cable slack and made sure the tray had room to move. Those checks connected the boundary conditions in the model to the assembled hardware.

Ground motor tests

We logged the IMU with the motors running on the ground for the rigid baseline and both isolation mounts. In MATLAB, I used the time histories to select comparable steady portions and avoid startup transients.

I compared root-mean-square (RMS) vibration by axis and used the fast Fourier transform (FFT) to see which frequencies contributed to it. RMS gave me an overall level for the selected window, and the spectra showed whether the change was concentrated at particular frequencies. Both isolation mounts reduced measured IMU vibration relative to the rigid baseline.

When reading the spectra, I considered that a peak could come from motor excitation, structural motion, or both. Comparing the same sensor axes across the mounts helped me focus on changes associated with the mounting arrangement.

The actual vibration measurements are confidential under the NDA.

Mount selection

We selected the elastomer-isolated tray after comparing the ground tests, ANSYS results, and installation checks. It kept the electronics together in a compact package and was easier to install consistently than the suspended arrangement. The model explained how the mounts could move, and the logs showed what reached the sensor in the aircraft.
